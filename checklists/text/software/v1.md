# Text checklist: software, v1

The text checklist of a software page: node.md, claims.yaml and derivations/.
Answer each question pass, fail or n/a; give a note for each fail. Yes means pass.

1. Does "What it is for" state the purpose and the users right, per the project's documentation?
2. Does "Core concepts" name the main abstractions in the vocabulary of the documentation, and explain each right?
3. Does the program of "Example" show the core concepts, and is the output shown the runner's output for the pinned version?
4. Does the example use only documented, non-deprecated APIs of the pinned version?
5. Does "Ecosystem and alternatives" compare within the same category, with a source for each difference and no ranking?
6. Does every claim in claims.yaml have the right type, with a scope (empirical) or assumptions (mathematical) no wider than its sources, and does no sentence of node.md state more than its claim?
7. Pick three claims of claims.yaml at random: does the cited source, at its locator, say what each claim says, within its scope?
8. Does History, for the releases that changed the field, name the right people, works and years, and is every "first" claim justified by its sources and clear about the sense of "first"?
9. Does node.md leave every judgement, rank and prediction to a named source or a computed fact, with nothing in the site voice that only a source should say, with no marketing words from the project's own site?
10. Does no passage of node.md read as copied or closely paraphrased from the project's README or documentation you know?
