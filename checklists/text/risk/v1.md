# Text checklist: risk, v1

The text checklist of a risk page: node.md, claims.yaml and derivations/.
Answer each question pass, fail or n/a; give a note for each fail. Yes means pass.

1. Does the lead say what the risk is and why it matters, in our words?
2. Is "In plain words" right, and can a reader follow it with no notation?
3. Does "Definition and scope" say what is inside and outside the risk, and give the competing definitions of the source taxonomies right?
4. Does "How it arises" explain the mechanism right, with an example that a source documents?
5. Is each item of "Examples" a documented demonstration or incident with a source, and none exaggerated?
6. Does the note of "Evidence" state frequency, severity and scale only as far as the claims support?
7. Is each item of "Mitigations" stated with the evidence for its effect, and none called effective beyond its claims?
8. Does "Debates" give each position on likelihood or severity in the same form, with sources?
9. Does node.md give no operational detail that would help cause the harm, beyond what a reader needs to understand it?
10. Does every claim in claims.yaml have the right type, with a scope (empirical) or assumptions (mathematical) no wider than its sources, and does no sentence of node.md state more than its claim?
11. Pick three claims of claims.yaml at random: does the cited source, at its locator, say what each claim says, within its scope?
12. Does "Examples" name the right people, works and years, and is every "first" claim justified by its sources and clear about the sense of "first"?
13. Does node.md leave every judgement, rank and prediction to a named source or a computed fact, with nothing in the site voice that only a source should say?
14. Does no passage of node.md read as copied or closely paraphrased from a source you know?
