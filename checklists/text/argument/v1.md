# Text checklist: argument, v1

The text checklist of a argument page: node.md, claims.yaml and derivations/.
Answer each question pass, fail or n/a; give a note for each fail. Yes means pass.

1. Does "In plain words" state the argument fairly, with no notation?
2. Are the premises and the conclusion of "The argument" numbered and in the strongest form that its proponents give, with their sources?
3. Would a proponent of the argument accept the statement of "The argument" as theirs?
4. Does "Context" say right what question the argument addresses and what it responds to?
5. Does "Replies" include every named reply that the literature treats as standing, with its proponents and sources?
6. Is each reply stated in a form its proponents would accept, with its main counter-replies and their sources?
7. Do the argument and each reply get the same parts and the same tone, with no adjective of praise or blame that another lacks?
8. Does "Influence" attribute each effect on research to a source?
9. Does every claim in claims.yaml have the right type, with a scope (empirical) or assumptions (mathematical) no wider than its sources, and does no sentence of node.md state more than its claim, and are attributive claims about who argues what, never about whether it is true?
10. Pick three claims of claims.yaml at random: does the cited source, at its locator, say what each claim says, within its scope?
11. Does node.md name the right people, works and years, and is every "first" claim justified by its sources and clear about the sense of "first"?
12. Does node.md take no side on whether the argument or any reply succeeds?
13. Does no passage of node.md read as copied or closely paraphrased from a source you know?
