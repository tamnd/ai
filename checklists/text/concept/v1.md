# Text checklist: concept, v1

The text checklist of a concept page: node.md, claims.yaml and derivations/.
Answer each question pass, fail or n/a; give a note for each fail. Yes means pass.

1. Does the lead say what the concept is and why it matters, in our words?
2. Is "In plain words" right, and can a reader follow it with no notation?
3. Does "The idea" give the smallest example that shows the concept, and is the example right?
4. Does "Formal statement" match the definition of its cited source, up to the notation map?
5. Do "Examples" include a non-example or a borderline case, and is each example classified right?
6. Does "Related ideas" state each difference right?
7. Is each item of "Common confusions" a misreading that the canon or the literature names, with its source?
8. Does every claim in claims.yaml have the right type, with a scope (empirical) or assumptions (mathematical) no wider than its sources, and does no sentence of node.md state more than its claim?
9. Pick three claims of claims.yaml at random: does the cited source, at its locator, say what each claim says, within its scope?
10. Does History name who named the concept, when and where, give the competing uses of the term, and justify every "first" claim?
11. Does node.md leave every judgement, rank and prediction to a named source or a computed fact, with nothing in the site voice that only a source should say?
12. Does no passage of node.md read as copied or closely paraphrased from a source you know?
