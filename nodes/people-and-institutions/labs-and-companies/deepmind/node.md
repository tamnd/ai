Google DeepMind is an artificial intelligence research unit of Google, formed in April 2023 from DeepMind and the Brain team from Google Research. {#c-unit}
Demis Hassabis writes that he founded DeepMind in 2010 with Shane Legg and Mustafa Suleyman. {#c-founded}
The Royal Society reported in November 2014 that Google had acquired DeepMind Technologies earlier that year. {#c-acquired}

## Overview

The company behind the lab, DEEPMIND TECHNOLOGIES LIMITED, is a private limited company with its registered office in London. {#c-company}
Companies House records that it was incorporated on 23 September 2010 under the name FRIARS 2022 LIMITED, which it used until 15 November 2010. {#c-incorporated}

## History

The sources differ on the year of founding.
Hassabis gives 2010, as quoted above, and again in his note announcing Google DeepMind. {#c-2010-again}
A Royal Society news item of 2014 states that Hassabis founded DeepMind Technologies in 2011 with partners Shane Legg and Mustafa Suleyman. {#c-2011}

The same news item reports that DeepMind Technologies and Hassabis's team were bought by Google for approximately £400 million. {#c-price}

In December 2019 Hassabis wrote that Mustafa Suleyman was leaving DeepMind to join Google in a new role. {#c-suleyman-leaves}

On 20 April 2023 Sundar Pichai announced a unit called Google DeepMind that brings together the Brain team from Google Research and DeepMind. {#c-merger}
Hassabis wrote to staff that the two would join forces as a single, focused unit. {#c-joining-forces}

## Work

The edges to the works are generated; the note below gives four landmark works in their authors' terms.

Mnih and colleagues (2015), at Google DeepMind in London, report a deep Q-network that receives only the pixels and the game score as inputs. {#c-dqn}
They report a level comparable to a professional human games tester across 49 Atari games, with the same algorithm, network architecture and hyperparameters. {#c-dqn-49}

Silver and colleagues (2016) describe AlphaGo, which combines Monte Carlo simulation with value and policy networks. {#c-alphago}
They report that AlphaGo won 99.8% of games against other Go programs and defeated the human European Go champion by 5 games to 0. {#c-alphago-results}

Jumper and colleagues (2021), at DeepMind in London, describe AlphaFold as the first computational method that can regularly predict protein structures with atomic accuracy even when no similar structure is known. {#c-alphafold}
They validated it in the 14th Critical Assessment of protein Structure Prediction (CASP14). {#c-casp14}

The Gemini report of December 2023, by the Gemini Team at Google, introduces a family of multimodal models across image, audio, video and text, in Ultra, Pro and Nano sizes. {#c-gemini}

## People

Pichai announced in April 2023 that Hassabis would lead Google DeepMind as its CEO. {#c-ceo}
In the same post he named Jeff Dean Chief Scientist of Google, serving Google Research and Google DeepMind. {#c-dean}
In December 2019 Hassabis described Shane Legg as DeepMind's Chief Scientist. {#c-legg}

On 9 October 2024 the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences awarded half of the Nobel Prize in Chemistry 2024 jointly to Hassabis and John Jumper, both of Google DeepMind, "for protein structure prediction". {#c-nobel}
The other half went to David Baker "for computational protein design". {#c-nobel-baker}
The Academy writes that in 2020 Hassabis and Jumper presented an AI model called AlphaFold2. {#c-alphafold2}
